📖 Help — tips & an example email

Tips for Contacting Professors

  • 1Time it right. Send emails Tue–Thu, 8–10 a.m. in the professor's time zone. Avoid holidays and the two weeks before admission deadlines, when inboxes overflow.
  • 2Read one real paper first. Cite a specific paper or project (paste its topic into the form). Generic flattery reads as spam; specificity reads as scholarship.
  • 3Attach a CV & unofficial transcript as PDFs, named Lastname_CV.pdf. Mention the attachments so they aren't overlooked.
  • 4Keep it under 150 words. Professors skim. One direct opener, one paragraph of fit, one line of logistics — then stop. The live counter above the draft watches this for you.
  • 5Follow up once after 7–10 days. Reply to your original message with a short, polite bump. No reply after that? Move on gracefully.
  • 6Use your institutional email if possible, and double-check the professor's title (Dr./Prof.) and spelling before sending. Personalize one email at a time — never BCC a cohort.

My CV is attached. Thank you for your time.

Best regards,
Alex Rivera

            Zoom Interview Cheat Sheet

            Five strategic questions to ask a professor during an informal 15-minute chat. Pick three — let the conversation breathe.

            1. 1Which project would a new student most likely be plugged into first, and what does success look like there in year one?
            2. 2How is that work funded right now — grant, studentship, or fellowship — and is that funding secured through my intended start term?
            3. 3How do you mentor students day-to-day: weekly one-on-ones, group meetings, hands-on with senior PhDs?
            4. 4What skills do your strongest recent students arrive with — and which should I sharpen before starting?
            5. 5Where do graduates of your group typically end up, and how visible will my name be on publications?

            Pro move: end with “What are the next steps on your side, and when should I follow up?” — then log the answer in your Outreach Tracker.
